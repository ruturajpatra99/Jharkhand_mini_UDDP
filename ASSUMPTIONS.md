# Assumptions and interpretation limits

2. **Estimated pension facts.** Every supplied district value is retained, not recomputed. `fact_pension_beneficiaries` has district/scheme grain (72 rows). Fields are named `estimated_*` and an estimate flag equals 1. The input's `Category` must be `District (estimated)`. The state rollup sums estimates; it is not a fourth independent source.
3. **Controls, not independent validation.** Expected totals 993,567/272,108/31,286 are supplied reconciliation controls. Matching them does not prove the district allocation correct. The three CSVs do not document its method or uncertainty intervals.
4. **Unknown pension time.** `reference_year` remains NULL. Ingestion date and logical execution date do not establish the observation year. Census is treated as 2011 according to the assessment. Cross-period comparisons are limited.
5. **Household units.** Housing `Total Number of households=100` is a percentage total, not an absolute count. `demographics.Households` is used for household counts with explicit provenance. Its aggregate is a sum of source-labelled values, not an independently verified Census household universe. Housing percentages are never multiplied by this count to fabricate housing-condition counts.
6. **Unsupported age/eligibility.** `Age_Group_50` does not establish an age-60+ denominator. Female population is not the population of eligible widows. Elderly share and eligibility-based old-age/widow coverage are NULL.
7. **Rates and units.** Scheme estimates divided by total population produce estimated entries per 1,000 residents. Dividing summed schemes by households produces estimated scheme entries per 1,000 households. It is not the percentage of unique households covered; cross-scheme overlap is unknown. The main two-scheme household metric includes old age and widow; an additional all-scheme metric includes disability and is clearly named.
8. **Literacy.** Literate/total population is a crude share. It is not the official literacy rate among people aged seven and above.
9. **Score policy.** 35/25/20/10/10 weights are an explainable design choice. They are not officially approved or fitted. Pension-derived contributions may reflect how estimates were allocated. A no-pension sensitivity scenario exposes this dependence. Disability counts are reported but not scored.
10. **Normalization.** Min-max normalization is relative to these 24 districts. Constant features contribute zero. Missing inputs fail. Exact ranks use rounded score descending and code ascending, so equal scores can land in different quota categories. Eight High, eight Medium and eight Low are comparison groups, not clinical/eligibility classifications.
11. **Snapshots and keys.** Codes are the Census 2011 district identifiers, not automatically current LGD codes. Tables represent the latest snapshot. Historical observations require period keys and boundary crosswalks; replaying logical dates does not fabricate a time series.
12. **Data quality.** Passing checks establish internal consistency and the enforced contract, not official truth. Exact duplicates are retained once with warning/rejection evidence; conflicts block publication. No source record disappears without accounting.
13. **Spark and SQLite.** Spark transformations are real, but local[2] is not a distributed-cluster benchmark. Driver collection is capped at 1,000 rows per table. SQLite uses a single writer and is an assessment serving layer, not a multi-department concurrent data warehouse.
14. **ML target.** `high_priority` is rule-derived and not an independent outcome. Score/category and their inputs create a circular learning task. No predictive model or accuracy claim is provided.
15. **Execution platform.** The pipeline is executed on Linux with Python 3.12.14, PySpark 3.5.6 and Java 17.0.20. Windows commands and code portability are provided.
16. **Privacy.** Only public district/state aggregates and explicitly separated synthetic test defects are used.
